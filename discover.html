Discover: Phase 1 of DEEDS

Where curiosity meets consciousness.

Students see, hear, and feel a challenge before they study it, building empathy, connection, and social consciousness. This creates a compelling personal "why" for the learning and action to come.

By the End

Success Criteria

By the end of Discover, students should demonstrate growth in three core skill areas:

1

Frame and Define the Challenge

Ask questions and use evidence to name a real-world problem worth investigating.

NGSS SEP 1, SEP 4 · C3 D4.6 · CCSS R.7
2

Understand Human Impact

Examine different perspectives to understand who is affected and how.

C3 D2.Civ.10 · CCSS R.6
3

Articulate Purpose & Significance

Make evidence-based claims about why the problem is important and what should change.

NGSS SEP 7 · CCSS W.1, W.9, SL.4
See How It Works

Student Experience

~2–5 sessions

Discover mirrors how people encounter, understand, and begin to care about real-world challenges, both in school and in the wider world.

The Four-Part Sequence
Step 1
Experience the Challenge

Meet the challenge through a provocation or real-world experience.

Step 2
Investigate Scale and Impact

Explore perspectives and evidence to understand who is affected and how big the problem is.

Step 3
Make Meaning

Reflect on what you've learned and how it connects to you or your community.

Step 4
Explain Why It Matters

Make an evidence-based claim about why the challenge is significant and worth solving.

Sample Discover Plans

Peaceful Paws

“How can we design a safe and calming toy that helps dogs in foster care feel more comfortable while they wait to be adopted?”
Grade BandK–2
Community PartnerMuddy Paws Rescue
Content AreaPhysical Science & Engineering
Discover Duration5 sessions

Muddy Paws Rescue cares for dogs through a network of foster homes while helping them find permanent families. Some dogs may feel nervous, overstimulated, or uncertain as they adjust to new people, places, routines, and adoption experiences. Muddy Paws asked students to help design something that could help dogs feel calmer while they wait to be adopted.

Nobody's Listening

“How can we help people understand the human impact of conflict so that stories that are often overlooked are not forgotten?”
Grade Band6
Community PartnerNobody's Listening (Ryan D'Souza)
Content AreaSocial Studies
Discover Duration5 sessions

Nobody's Listening asked students to help build an exhibit that helps visitors understand and connect with the human impact of conflicts most people know little about. Working in cycles, students develop the first part of the exhibit: interactive experiences that help visitors see the people and places behind conflicts in the Eastern Hemisphere, so their stories are not forgotten.

Crowd Power: The Physics of Protest

“How can we use physics to help people participate in public demonstrations more safely and effectively?”
Grade Band9–10
Community PartnerNYCLU
Content AreaPhysics
Discover Duration2 sessions

When large groups gather to march, speak out, or demand justice, things can get risky, especially when space is tight, emotions run high, and systems aren't designed with safety in mind. The NYCLU asked students to investigate protest dynamics and design real solutions that help people move together more safely during collective action.

Grounded in the Science of Learning and Development

In Discover, students build value, meaningful encoding, constructive emotions, self-understanding, and connection to their own life experiences.

They immerse themselves in a real community challenge through site visits, interviews, and observations, encountering distinctive, emotionally compelling moments that anchor new learning in firsthand experience. A real-world client gives them an ongoing reason to care about the challenge. When students reflect on why the challenge matters to them personally, they link their own stories and communities to the work. They also build relationships with community partners, introduced here and continued through later phases, gaining access to resources beyond the classroom.
