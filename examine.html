Examine: Phase 2 of DEEDS

Where questions meet evidence.

Students investigate the root causes of the challenge through disciplinary inquiry, building the content knowledge and evidence base needed to propose and defend a solution direction.

See How It Works

Student Experience

~13–17 sessions

During Examine, students engage in cycles of disciplinary inquiry. They generate questions, build knowledge, analyze evidence, and develop a defensible, evidence-based solution pathway.

The Three-Part Sequence
Step 1
Frame Your Questions

Generate investigable questions that will help you understand and address the challenge.

Step 2
Investigate & Apply Evidence

Build disciplinary knowledge through texts, labs, and data in service of understanding the challenge's root causes and crafting effective solutions.

Step 3
Deliberate & Commit to a Solution Direction

Deliberate about competing solution possibilities through structured discussion, then commit to the most defensible direction.

By the End

Success Criteria

By the end of Examine, students should demonstrate growth in three areas of disciplinary reasoning and evidence use:

1

Apply Disciplinary Concepts

Accurately apply targeted content standards to explain how the system behind the challenge operates and what causes the problem.

NGSS SEP 2, SEP 6 · CCSS R.1, R.4, W.2 · C3 D2
2

Identify & Analyze Root Causes

Use disciplinary evidence to identify underlying causes, analyze impact and magnitude, and compare competing explanations.

NGSS SEP 4, SEP 6 · CCSS W.1 · C3 D3
3

Evaluate & Justify Solutions

Compare solution pathways using criteria and evidence, then defend a solution direction by synthesizing findings across all instructional cycles.

NGSS SEP 7, ETS1-2 · CCSS W.1, W.9, SL.4 · C3 D4.6
Grounded in the Science of Learning and Development

In Examine, students build manageable cognitive load, meaningful encoding, effective practice, and sense of control as they work through the challenge in distinct, purposeful steps.

They connect new content to the prior experience and the challenge they witnessed firsthand in Discover, building organizing schemas that link concepts across disciplines. They practice literacy and reasoning skills through research at the edge of their comfort zone, and they exercise real agency by choosing which questions to investigate.
