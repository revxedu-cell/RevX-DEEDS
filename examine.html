Examine: Phase 2 of DEEDS

Where questions meet evidence.

Students investigate the root causes of the challenge through disciplinary inquiry, building the content knowledge and evidence base needed to propose and defend a solution direction.

Grounded in the Science of Learning

Chunking the challenge into distinct steps supports manageable cognitive load, and connecting new content to firsthand experience strengthens meaningful encoding. Along the way, students build effective practice and exercise sense of control over their own questions.

By the End

Success Criteria

By the end of Examine, students should demonstrate growth in three areas of disciplinary reasoning and evidence use:

1

Apply Disciplinary Concepts

Accurately apply targeted content standards to explain how the system behind the challenge operates and what causes the problem.

NGSS SEP 2, SEP 6 · CCSS R.1, R.4, W.2 · C3 D2
2

Identify & Analyze Root Causes

Use disciplinary evidence to identify underlying causes, analyze impact and magnitude, and compare competing explanations.

NGSS SEP 4, SEP 6 · CCSS W.1 · C3 D3
3

Evaluate & Justify Solutions

Compare solution pathways using criteria and evidence, then defend a solution direction by synthesizing findings across all instructional cycles.

NGSS SEP 7, ETS1-2 · CCSS W.1, W.9, SL.4 · C3 D4.6
See How It Works

Student Experience

~13–17 sessions

During Examine, students engage in cycles of disciplinary inquiry. They generate questions, build knowledge, analyze evidence, and develop a defensible, evidence-based solution pathway.

The Three-Part Sequence
Step 1
Frame Your Questions

Generate investigable questions that will help you understand and address the challenge.

Step 2
Investigate & Apply Evidence

Build disciplinary knowledge through texts, labs, and data in service of understanding the challenge's root causes and crafting effective solutions.

Step 3
Deliberate & Commit to a Solution Direction

Deliberate about competing solution possibilities through structured discussion, then commit to the most defensible direction.

Sample Examine Plans

“How can we design a safe and calming toy that helps dogs in foster care feel more comfortable while they wait to be adopted?”
Community PartnerMuddy Paws Rescue
Content AreaPhysical Science & Engineering
Examine Duration8 sessions

Students investigate the science behind dog anxiety, observing and testing materials, classifying their properties, and analyzing evidence to determine which materials are best suited for a calming toy.

“How can we help people understand the human impact of conflict so that stories that are often overlooked are not forgotten?”
Community PartnerNobody's Listening (Ryan D'Souza)
Content AreaSocial Studies
Examine Duration19 sessions

Students build map literacy, then research their region's conflict across three cycles: establishing geographic context, investigating historical roots, and identifying the deeper factors that keep the conflict going.

“How can we use physics to help people participate in public demonstrations more safely and effectively?”
Community PartnerNYCLU
Content AreaPhysics
Examine Duration6 sessions

Students use Newton's Laws, momentum, and impulse to explain how people and objects move and collide in crowded spaces, applying their understanding to evaluate safety risks and begin designing a solution.
