Evidence of Learning
Students are ready to move on from Do when they can demonstrate the following:
Success Criteria
1
Enact a Solution
Implement the solution with real users, systems, or environments; document the process; and adapt when real-world conditions require it.
2
Collect & Use Evidence
Gather relevant data about impact and effectiveness using appropriate tools — measurements, observations, surveys — and organize findings clearly.
3
Evaluate Impact & Effectiveness
Use evidence to explain what worked, what didn’t, and why — identifying limitations, tradeoffs, unintended consequences, and meaningful next steps.
Students should be able to respond thoroughly and thoughtfully to the following prompts:
1
What happened when you tried your solution? Did it help?
2
What did you find out? What surprised you?
3
What would you change if you tried again?
1
Did your solution work? What evidence shows it helped — or didn’t?
2
What happened that you didn’t expect? Why do you think that happened?
3
What would you improve or do differently next time?
1
What did the evidence show about your solution’s impact? Use data or observations to support your analysis.
2
What worked, what didn’t, and why? Explain at least one limitation or unexpected result.
3
What improvements or next steps would make the solution more effective?
1
Evaluate your solution’s impact: what does the evidence show about effectiveness, and how confident are you in these findings?
2
What tradeoffs, limitations, or unintended consequences did you encounter? How do they affect your conclusions?
3
What should happen next? Make an evidence-based recommendation for how the solution could be improved or scaled.
