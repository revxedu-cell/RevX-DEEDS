2-PS1-1 · 2-PS1-2 · 2-PS1-3 · K-2-ETS1-1 · K-2-ETS1-2 · K-2-ETS1-3
Discover Sessions 1–5

Students meet anxious dogs in foster care through a visit from a Muddy Paws Rescue worker and build a shared understanding of the challenge, who is affected, and why it matters.

Examine Sessions 6–13

Students investigate the science behind dog anxiety, observing and testing materials, classifying their properties, and analyzing foster care data to determine which materials are best suited for a calming toy.

Engineer Sessions 14–18

Students design, build, and refine calming toys for dogs in foster care, selecting materials based on evidence, planning their build, and making revisions based on testing and peer feedback.

Do Sessions 19–21

Students bring their calming toys to dogs in foster care, observe how the dogs respond, and use their evidence to evaluate what worked, what didn't, and what they would do differently.

Share Sessions 22–25

Students present their calming toys and learning to Muddy Paws Rescue and invited families, explaining what they discovered, how their solution was designed, and what the evidence showed.

3-5-ETS1-1 · 4-PS3-1 · 4-PS3-3 · 4-PS4-1 · 4-PS4-3
Discover Days 1–3

Learners took part in a walk audit near their school, observing driver behavior and intersection conditions. Experts from Transportation Alternatives and the Center for Architecture helped students understand why intersections needed redesign for pedestrian safety.

Examine Days 4–14

Students investigated how speeding impacts pedestrian safety through crash simulations and real-world traffic data analysis, building a conceptual understanding of energy transfer through explicit NGSS instruction.

Engineer Days 15–24

Working with Center for Architecture mentors, students crafted detailed 3D models of safer intersections and built companion websites explaining the science behind their designs.

Do Days 25–28

Learners launched their websites and tracked petition signatures as support for the NYC Streets Plan grew, measuring real civic impact from fourth graders.

Share Days 29–30

Students reflected on their roles as Transportation Engineers, sharing their growth and celebrating the connection between their science learning and real community change.

MS-PS1-1 · MS-PS1-2 · MS-PS1-3 · MS-ESS3-3 · MS-ESS3-4
Discover Days 1–5

Students met with FabScrap, a nonprofit textile recycling warehouse, and analyzed data on the environmental, labor, and public health impacts of fast fashion, then collected donated clothing as raw materials.

Examine Days 6–21

Learners conducted scientific investigations into materials science and fabric chemistry (dyeing, bleaching, shrinking, fraying) and wrote CER arguments recommending materials for real design scenarios.

Engineer Days 22–27

Working alongside fashion design grad students, learners constructed upcycled garments from donated clothing, applying materials science while preparing pieces for the runway.

Do Days 28–29

Students launched their runway show for peers, families, and community members, then collected data on how audience attitudes toward fashion shifted.

Share Days 30–32

Learners reflected on their identities as materials scientists and sustainable designers, completing a summative science assessment celebrating real-world impact.

RI 11.1 · RI 11.4 · RI 11.6
Discover Days 1–3

Students named mental health as a community issue through a gallery walk, videos, and trend data, then connected with NAMI Mississippi, whose Let's Talk 988 session introduced the crisis hotline.

Examine Days 4–14

Students researched Mississippi's strained mental health system, wrote informative reports, and identified target audiences: student athletes, college-bound seniors, and teens affected by social media.

Engineer Days 15–24

Learners designed multi-modal campaign materials (videos, flyers, and social media content), each group developing a distinct approach for its target audience.

Do Days 24–30

Students hosted events across the county, including a session for athletes on anxiety and pressure in sports. Over 100 students attended and were surveyed before and after.

Share Days 28–30

Learners shared their work directly with school board members, an act of civic leadership that led to multiple district-sponsored mental health events across the community.

Discover

Students meet representatives from Precious Dreams Foundation, explore what comfort means, and listen to age-appropriate stories about children entering shelters, foster care, or hospitals.

Examine

Students investigate how light, sound, texture, temperature, and familiar routines can affect how people feel, then compare materials and existing comfort objects.

Engineer

Students design and build comfort items or kits, selecting materials based on safety, sensory properties, durability, and the needs of the children who will use them.

Do

Students provide their completed kits to the partner organization and gather feedback about which items are most useful, comforting, and appropriate.

Share

Students explain their design choices to families and partners, reflect on how they showed care for others, and describe what they learned about materials and human needs.

4-PS3-1 · 4-PS3-2 · 4-PS3-4 · 4-ESS3-1 · 3-5-ETS1-1 · 3-5-ETS1-2 · 3-5-ETS1-3
Discover

Students complete a school energy walk, analyze how electricity is used, and meet sustainability professionals who challenge them to identify opportunities for cleaner energy.

Examine

Students investigate energy transfer, circuits, motion, and renewable energy systems through hands-on experiments, data analysis, and scientific modeling.

Engineer

Teams design and construct working devices, such as kinetic floor tiles or bicycle-powered generators, using evidence to justify their materials, mechanisms, and intended uses.

Do

Students install or demonstrate their devices in a realistic school setting and collect measurements to determine how much energy they produce under different conditions.

Share

Students present their prototypes and performance data to engineers, school leaders, and families, explaining how their scientific understanding shaped each design decision.

MS-PS3-1 · MS-PS3-3 · MS-PS3-4 · MS-PS3-5 · MS-PS3-6
Discover

Students investigate refrigerated truck traffic and air-quality concerns, analyze local pollution data, and hear from community members and professionals affected by the challenge.

Examine

Students study thermal energy transfer, insulation, energy systems, emissions, and the constraints involved in transporting temperature-sensitive goods.

Engineer

Teams design and construct low-emission refrigeration prototypes, selecting materials and energy sources based on performance, cost, safety, and environmental impact.

Do

Students test their prototypes under controlled conditions, measure temperature changes over time, and compare their results with existing approaches.

Share

Students present their findings to environmental or transportation professionals, defend their designs with scientific evidence, and recommend next steps for continued development.

HS-PS2-1 · HS-PS2-2 · HS-PS2-3
Discover

Students examine real protest environments and hear from organizers about challenges related to crowd movement, communication, visibility, accessibility, and safety.

Examine

Students investigate forces, motion, momentum, energy, and wave behavior through laboratory investigations connected to specific problems encountered during public demonstrations.

Engineer

Teams design and build tools such as portable communication systems, safer crowd barriers, visibility devices, or accessibility supports that apply physics to a documented user need.

Do

Students test their designs in realistic crowd scenarios, collect performance data and user feedback, and analyze how conditions affect safety and effectiveness.

Share

Students demonstrate their solutions to organizers or civil-rights partners, defend their design choices using physics, and reflect on the relationship between technical design and civic participation.

6.2 · 6.3 · 6.4
Discover

Students experience an immersive exhibition about communities affected by conflict and examine how war is represented differently by witnesses, governments, journalists, and historians.

Examine

Students study historical context, primary sources, oral histories, and media accounts to analyze causes, consequences, perspective, and the reliability of evidence.

Engineer

Working as historians and experience designers, students create virtual or multimedia exhibits that combine accurate historical evidence with carefully chosen stories and artifacts.

Do

Students invite peers and community members to experience the exhibits and collect feedback about what audiences learned, felt, questioned, or reconsidered.

Share

Students defend their curatorial decisions, reflect on the responsibilities involved in representing other people's experiences, and explain how their historical thinking changed.
