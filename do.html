Do: Phase 4 of DEEDS

Where solutions come to life.

Students put their solution into action in the real world, collect evidence of what happens, and use data to evaluate its impact and effectiveness.

See How It Works

Student Experience

~2–5 sessions

In the Do phase, students implement their solution in the real world. They take action and gather and analyze evidence of the solution's impact and effectiveness.

Step 1
Plan Your Implementation

Define how and where the solution will be enacted and how evidence will be collected.

Step 2
Enact & Document Evidence

Put your solution into action and systematically collect data, observations, and user feedback as evidence of what's happening.

Step 3
Analyze & Improve

Examine what the evidence shows about your solution's effectiveness, identify what worked and what didn't, and determine what should change next.

By the End

Success Criteria

By the end of Do, students should demonstrate growth across three areas of real-world implementation and evidence-based evaluation:

1

Enact a Solution

Implement the solution with real users, systems, or environments, document the process, and adapt when conditions on the ground require it.

NGSS ETS1-3 · CCSS W.2, SL.4 · C3 D4.7
2

Collect & Use Evidence

Gather relevant data about impact and effectiveness using appropriate tools (measurements, observations, surveys) and organize findings clearly.

NGSS SEP 4, SEP 5 · CCSS W.7 · MP.4
3

Evaluate Impact & Effectiveness

Use evidence to explain what worked, what didn't, and why, identifying limitations, tradeoffs, unintended consequences, and meaningful next steps.

NGSS SEP 6, SEP 7 · CCSS W.1, W.9 · C3 D4.6
Grounded in the Science of Learning and Development

In Do, students build high-quality feedback, sense of control, and responsiveness to individual variability as they move into real-world implementation.

When they put their solution into practice, they get authentic feedback about whether it works, more meaningful than any simulated test. They develop agency over external factors by working to create real change, seeing firsthand that their actions can influence outcomes that matter. Because there's no single right solution in Do, students bring different approaches and backgrounds that lead to varied, often unexpected results.
